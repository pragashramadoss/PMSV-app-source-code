## New immediate findings

1. **Coconut milk powder**, FSSR 2.3.63: source explicitly *permits* sodium caseinate and maltodextrin in formulations and specifies minimum **60% fat (dry basis)**. Calling the category exclusively dairy-free without checking actual caseinate is unsafe. It is still not ordinary fluid milk or dairy milk powder for a lead contaminant transfer.
2. **Vending-machine purified drinking water**, FSSR 2.10.9: expressly excludes packaged drinking and natural mineral water in the definition; it references **IS 10500**. A packaged-water lead table is not automatically the correct as-sold CTR article.
3. **Coffee–chicory**, 2.10.4: not less than **51% coffee** in specified blends, with distinct variants. This says nothing conclusive about a coffee-only ochratoxin A limit for a mixed commercial food.
4. **Cocoa beans**, 2.3.54: fermented dried **whole** cacao seeds, not automatically cocoa powder on dry-fat-free basis.
5. **Dried glucose syrup**, 2.8.7: sulphated ash **≤1.0%**. A comparator demanding >1.0% sulphated ash cannot match an otherwise conforming lot; independently establish the correct lead article.
6. **Misri**, 2.8.2: **total ash** ≤0.4% is not sulphated ash; crystal candy is not automatically refined sugar for chromium.
7. **Carob**, 2.11.7: ground roasted **carob pods**, not cocoa powder.
8. **Edible lactose**, 2.1.20: whey-derived disaccharide **≥99% dry basis**, not automatically whole fluid or dried dairy milk for lead.
9. **Silver leaf**, 2.11.4: minimum **999/1000 silver fineness** is a product identity/quality criterion, not a finished-product lead limit.
10. **Dextrin-soluble fibre**, 2.11.8: distinct glucose polymer, not solid glucose or high-ash sugars.

The other **21 open cases** retain prior evidence leads and remain in a separate filter pending new direct source review. No claim that the prior supporting material is lost or disproved.
