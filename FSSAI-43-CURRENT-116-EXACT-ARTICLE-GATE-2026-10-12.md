## Critical source conditions and protections

1. **Use precise analyte units.** Natural toxins in ppm; aflatoxins in µg/kg; metals in mg/kg. The source row for tin of *Mango Chutney* and *Pickled cucumber* has no can-only condition, unlike separately worded canned fruits/mushrooms.
2. **Prohibit blanket cross-product transfers.** Edible fungi are not always mushrooms. Industrial vegetable puree is not always tomato concentrate. Anhydrous, refined white and high-ash raw sugars have different lead article scopes. Indian sweets are not automatically hard-boiled confectionery.
3. **Check dried solids and ash method.** Tomato copper 100 mg/kg expressly refers to dried tomato solids, not wet tomato puree. Sugar high ash is **sulphated ash >1%**, not total ash; dried glucose syrup has conflicting product-specification ash maximum.
4. **Retain the current first-source legal review status.** Every source route needs actual finished-product identity, recipe/species, processing type, package, sampling, regulatory amendment and laboratory records. Choosing an option in PMSV is a *research declaration*, not actual evidence.
5. **Fail closed.** A local dual-stream snapshot fingerprint detects any post-load changes in the 45 article rows, 53 proof branches, numerical values, review flags and source URLs. The current 533-ledger snapshot guard must independently pass.
6. **Keep legal and technical claims separate.** The 43-case source material establishes that *those named source articles* exist in FSSAI Version IX. It does not adjudicate universal finished-product scope or establish complete pesticide/veterinary/microbiological coverage. Full compliance assessment still requires authorised legal and lab verification.
