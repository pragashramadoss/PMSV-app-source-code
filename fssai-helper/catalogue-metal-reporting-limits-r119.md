# R119 · Metal reporting limits and units

Under **Check a reported metal result**, enter the value as printed on the laboratory report, choose its unit and confirm the sampled article and analytical basis. The worksheet accepts an exact value, a strict upper bound such as `<10`, or an inclusive upper bound such as `≤10` or `<=10`. It keeps the original result in the product brief, register and individual testing plan. For microgram units it also shows the equivalent in milligrams per kilogram or per litre, retaining the denominator.

| Sample and reported result | Comparison on the documented basis |
| --- | --- |
| Natural mineral water, arsenic `<10 µg/L` | The upper bound is 0.01 mg/L, the cited maximum. Every value below that bound is within the maximum. |
| Same sample, arsenic `<20 µg/L` | Inconclusive. The actual concentration could lie on either side of the maximum. |
| Infant milk food, copper `<2800 µg/kg` | Every possible concentration is below the 2.8 mg/kg minimum. An inclusive bound of `≤2800` leaves equality possible and the finding inconclusive. |
| Fish oil, total arsenic `<100 µg/kg`, assessed from 1 December 2026 | The report establishes a concentration strictly below the screening threshold. A result of `≤100 µg/kg` leaves equality possible and the screen inconclusive. |

A report of `ND` or `<LOQ` without a number needs the laboratory's reporting threshold. Switching between per-kilogram and per-litre units clears the basis confirmation for that reading. A volume result for a mass-only criterion needs a separate assessment of the basis. A metal comparison covers the selected analyte in the documented sample. Lot release remains a separate QA decision.
