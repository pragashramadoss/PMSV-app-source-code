## High-value official-source distinctions

- **Dairy analogues (8):** Chapter 2.1, 2.1.1(1)(aa) requires replacement of milk constituents with non-milk constituents and resemblance to a milk product. FSSAI's 2022 dairy FAQ explicitly excludes wholly plant-based coconut milk from the analogue definition. Generic milk contaminant articles may not be inherited merely from an analogue label.
- **Vegetable-fat/protein frozen dessert (2):** Chapter 2.1, 2.1.15 is a separate identity standard. Exact application of the CTR phrase 'similar frozen confections' still needs legal article adjudication; no unverified exemption is claimed.
- **Coconut kernel milk, cream, powder (3):** Chapter 2.3 sections 2.3.51, 2.3.52 and 2.3.63. Sodium caseinate/maltodextrin can be permitted in certain product formulations; determine actual recipe. Do not automatically inherit dairy-milk, coconut-water or coconut-oil CTR articles.
- **Colostrum (1):** Chapter 2.1 section 2.1.23 provides a separate standard; cow/buffalo colostrum and its powder are not automatically ordinary milk.
- **Water chestnut flour (1):** Chapter 2.3 section 2.3.64 is dried Trapa nuts, not root/tuber or canned chestnut.
- **Purified vending water (1):** Chapter 2.10 section 2.10.9 references IS 10500 and distinguishes vending-machine water from packaged/natural mineral water.
- **Edible dried seed licensing (2):** FSSAI Order dated 15 March 2024 establishes interim FoSCoS licensing routes; it is not a numeric finished-product contaminant standard.
- **Cocoa beans / carob powder (2):** The CTR table's 'Cocoa powder' lead article has a dry fat-free substance basis. It cannot be automatically applied to whole cocoa beans or carob powder.
