## FSSR 2.9.29 — do not collapse all asafoetida into one test specification

| Type | Total ash maximum | Acid-insoluble ash maximum | Alcoholic extract minimum (90% alcohol, USP 1936) | Starch maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Hing** | 15% | 2.5% | 12% | 1% |
| **Hingra** | 20% | 8% | 50% | 1% |
| **Compounded asafoetida / Bandhani Hing** | 10% | 1.5% | 5% | Not separately printed among compounded restrictions |

The source standard defines the gum as an oleogumresin from specified **Ferula** root/rhizome species. It prohibits foreign resins, and compounded asafoetida additionally prohibits coal-tar dyes and mineral pigment. The actual source identity, compounded starch/cereal content, ingredients and specifications must be established before assigning any of the above.

*Source: official FPSFA Chapter 2.9 Version 3 (07.05.2025), PDF pages **34–35**, image checked.*

## FSSR 2.9.31 — Seasoning does not mean one generic spice limit

| Test | Published source value | Qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Moisture | Maximum **10%** by weight | Starred exception for seasoning pastes such as tomato, ginger, garlic or chilli |
| Ash insoluble in dilute HCl | Maximum **2%**, dry basis | This is **not** marked with the same paste exception |

The standard permits combinations of spices, herbs, salt, vegetables, starch, fats, dairy, soy, nuts, seafood and other legally specified foods. Their ingredient-specific permissions, allergens and applicable microbiological criteria must be reviewed. It also allows seasoning to be packed with another product or supplied separately; do not assume a sachet inherits the host food's sole identity.

*Source: FSSAI Chapter 2.9, PDF pages **39–40**, visually inspected.*

## FSSR 2.9.32 — Spice oleoresin extraction source

The specified permitted solvent residues are **not a generic single ppm limit**:

| Permitted solvent | Printed maximum |
|---|---:|
| Acetone | 30 ppm |
| Ethyl acetate | 50 ppm |
| n-Hexane | 25 ppm |
| Isopropyl alcohol | 30 ppm |
| Methyl alcohol | 50 ppm |
| Diethyl ether | 2 ppm |
| Butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol (each) | 2 ppm |
| Propan-1-ol | 1 ppm |
| Methyl tert-butyl ether | 2 ppm |
| Carbon dioxide, water and ethyl alcohol | **GMP**, not a fixed printed ppm |

The standard also provides **spice-specific volatile oil content (VOC) minimums in ml/100 g**. Examples visually confirmed: Ajowan **1.5**, Allspice **20.0**. Chilli/capsicum has a **dash**, not an automatic 0.0 minimum or unconditional PASS. This audit does not claim the VOC table is fully reconciled for every individual spice, or that all extraction routes and laboratories are approved.

*Source: FSSAI Chapter 2.9, PDF pages **40–41**, image checked.*

## CTR Version IX — candidate articles, not automatically selected limits

The official 3 February 2026 contaminants compendium (PDF pages **3 and 14**, visually checked) prints these *distinct* examples:

| Parameter / printed food article | Example limit |
|---|---:|
| Lead — dried herbs/spices, curry powder/mix masalas/flavourings and listed other sources | **10 mg/kg, dry matter** |
| Total aflatoxins — spices/spice mix | **30 µg/kg** |
| Aflatoxin B1 — spices/spice mix | **15 µg/kg** |
| Total aflatoxins — foods containing any of the named source articles | **20 µg/kg** |
| Aflatoxin B1 — foods containing any of the named source articles | **10 µg/kg** |

No example is automatically applicable to a compounded hing, a complex seasoning or a solvent-extracted oleoresin. For each actual SKU, choose the legally correct food article, effective amendment, matrix, processing factors, full contaminant/pesticide/microbiology panels, sample basis and validated analytical method; do not select a favourable value or infer an exemption.
