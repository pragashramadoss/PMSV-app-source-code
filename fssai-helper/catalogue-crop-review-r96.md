# R96 · FSSAI crop contaminants, natural toxins and fish contaminants

Edition reviewed on 20 November 2026. Each of the 533 catalogue worksheets and each CSV row covers the 12 analytes in the three tables of CTR 2.2.1. The 45 current Gazette rows comprise 27 crop-contaminant entries, 15 naturally occurring toxic-substance entries and 3 PCB/PAH entries. The register distinguishes what the marketed article establishes from what a recipe, species, process or ingredient sample can establish.

An unmatched identity is a finding about the cited tables and stated product description. Food-specific standards, other CTR sections and changes to an actual formulation can still supply criteria. The published register shows the baseline catalogue findings. In the Helper, a documented choice updates its product brief and CSV export. Each decision records one selected sample at a time. Ingredient-only findings stay visibly tied to the supplied material. The December preview is separate from the November assessment.

## Examples

| Product or sample | Current finding |
|---|---|
| Hard candy and soft candy | Confectionery carries a hydrocyanic acid maximum of 5 ppm. For finished candy containing a listed nut or spice, the applicable composite-food entries are 20 µg/kg for total aflatoxins and 10 µg/kg for B1. The worksheet asks for the formulation. |
| Nutmeg sold as the spice | Total aflatoxins 30 µg/kg and B1 15 µg/kg under the spice/spice-mix entry. |
| Milk powder and cream powder catalogue group | Select the actual grade. The aflatoxin M1 entry for skimmed milk powder is 6 µg/kg and for whole milk powder it is 4 µg/kg. Liquid milk has a separate maximum of 0.5 µg/kg. |
| Apple juice and cider | Apple juice carries a patulin maximum of 50 µg/kg. Where cider uses apple juice, the separate ingredient entry applies to the sampled juice. |
| Wheat bran, coffee and biscuit | Wheat bran has limits for ochratoxin A (5 µg/kg) and DON (1,000 µg/kg). Coffee is also named under ochratoxin A. A biscuit can use several different flours. Its wheat-input selection records an incoming-sample decision. |
| Smoked fishery product | Benzo(a)pyrene is 5 ppb in the smoked article. For the sum of the six indicator PCBs the entries are 2 ppm for inland or migratory fish and 0.5 ppm for marine fish, crustaceans and molluscs. The sample and species determine the route. |
| Bean | The separate bean standard 2.3.48 provides a 20 ppm hydrocyanic acid maximum for its defined seeds on the specified analytical basis. This cross-reference sits outside the 45 CTR rows. |
| Tomato sauce | The current saffrole maximum is 10 ppm for soups and sauces. From 1 December 2026 the Gazette replaces all six current saffrole entries with one ingredient-based entry. Check the actual use of mace and/or nutmeg kernel for that assessment. |

## The 12 priority products with named finished limits

| Products | Regulatory basis |
|---|---|
| Vanilla pods, cut vanilla, vanilla powder | Standard 2.3.50 defines the three forms of the same vanilla spice. The standalone spice entries govern total aflatoxins and B1. [FSSAI product standard, PDF p. 75](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_3_Fruit%20%20Vegetable%20products.pdf#page=75) · [Spices Board schedule](https://www.indianspices.com/right-to-information/particulars-its-organisation-functions-and-duties.html) |
| Protein-rich refined wheat flour, bread and bread-type products | The relevant standards define the wheat flour or wheat-flour bakery article. The cereal-product entries govern total aflatoxins and B1. [FSSAI chapter 2.4, PDF p. 4](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_4_Cereals_and_Cereal_products.pdf#page=4) · [PDF p. 56](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_4_Cereals_and_Cereal_products.pdf#page=56) |
| Malt-based food, tempe | The first standard specifies cereal malt. The second requires soybean and a starter combined with a listed cereal material. Both finished foods contain a listed article. [FSSAI chapter 2.4, PDF p. 36](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_4_Cereals_and_Cereal_products.pdf#page=36) · [PDF p. 70](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_4_Cereals_and_Cereal_products.pdf#page=70) |
| Lajadi Kashaya, Krishara, Yava Ksheera, Dugdha Kupika, Kulmasha | The recipes specify parched rice in the drink, cooked rice and pulse in the khichdi, barley flour in the milk, rice flour in the dumpling dough, and barley flour or a pulse in the discs. The Helper links the recipe image pages 84, 88, 94, 95 and 102 in the official PDF. Lajadi Kashaya and Yava Ksheera are also identified as drinks in their recipes. [FSSAI Ayurveda Aahara order and recipes](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Order%20dated%2025-07-2025%20enclosing%20Ayurveda%20Aahara.pdf#page=84) |
