## Jati and the Indian dried-herb article

Codex CXA 4-1989, amended in 2025, places *Jasminum officinale* L. at HH 3231 in subgroup 027A. The jasmine line leaves the part unspecified. Group 027 includes flowers used in small amounts to flavour food and recognises naturally dried forms. Its pesticide sampling description refers to the whole commodity as marketed, mainly fresh. Group 057 separately describes generally artificially dried, often comminuted herbs. These are commodity classifications for pesticide work. The Indian lead table supplies the numerical value and reporting basis. [Codex herb group, PDF p. 105](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=105) · [Jasmine, p. 109](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=109) · [Dried herbs, p. 218](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=218)

Both the original Gazette and the February 2026 FSSAI compendium explicitly name dried herbs in the following two metal entries. Applying them to authenticated, naturally dried culinary Jati flowers is an interpretation. The pages refer to the respective PDF files.

A fresh flower, an oil or extract, and an unidentified supply require separate assessment. The aflatoxin rows considered in the Hingu review name spices or spice mix. The herb classification supplies no independent basis for assigning them to jasmine flowers. No Indian or Codex pesticide MRL is assigned by this route. Review the sampled material, laboratory basis, current applicable article and any competing provisions before using a result. [Gazette crop-contaminant table, PDF p. 29](https://www.fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/notice-comment/5f3d09f97b78aGazette_Notification_Limit_Metal_19_08_2020.pdf#page=29)

## Ketaki and other possible source mix-ups

Codex names *Pandanus fascicularis* Lam. at HS 3379 for kewra flowers. Kew lists that name under *Pandanus odorifer* and distinguishes *P. tectorius* in its medicinal plant names service. Codex FI 2526 includes *P. tectorius* in a fruit entry and HH 3246 identifies *P. amaryllifolius* leaves. Each fails either the species or the plant-part comparison for the 14.6 flower. The recipe gives no basis for transferring a spice limit from those records. [Codex kewra, PDF p. 124](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=124) · [Screwpine fruit, p. 36](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=36) · [Pandan leaves, p. 110](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=110) · [Kew species record](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A670953-1) · [Kew common-name comparison](https://mpns.science.kew.org/mpns-portal/drugDetail?drugName=kewda)

Under regulation 4, Schedule C entry 28 of the 2022 Ayurveda Aahara Gazette lists **0.5% maximum for Kewda/Kewra oil from Ketaki**. It is an additive-use figure. The entry supplies neither a recipe-flower quantity nor a contaminant limit. Flower water and substitution of oil for the recipe flower each require their own review. [FSSAI regulation 4, PDF p. 16](https://www.fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/notice-comment/62789a20b54bdGazette_Notification_Ayurveda_Aahara_09_05_2022.pdf#page=16) · [Schedule C entry 28, PDF p. 22](https://www.fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/notice-comment/62789a20b54bdGazette_Notification_Ayurveda_Aahara_09_05_2022.pdf#page=22)

Codex FI 0355 identifies pomegranate under the fruit group. The original Indian pesticide table, serial 64, lists pomegranate under Metiram expressed as CS₂. That row supplies no flower-specific basis for Dadima. The other unresolved material, Punnaga, requires an article for the actual flower. Removing visible flowers during preparation does not establish the amount of any residue transferred to milk or water. [Codex pomegranate, PDF p. 33](https://codex.fao.org/restapi/searchstandard/CXA_004e.pdf?lang=en&id=39#page=33) · [FSSAI pesticide table, PDF p. 38](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Gazette_Notification_MRL_Pesticides_03_01_2019.pdf#page=38)
