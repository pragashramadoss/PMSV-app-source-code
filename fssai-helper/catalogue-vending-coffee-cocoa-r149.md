# Vending water, coffee–chicory and cocoa sample guide

The review on 26 November 2026 identified a time-limited contaminant reference for plain water dispensed from a vending machine. The catalogue has 524 products with at least one partial or conditional finished-product source on that date. On 1 December the saffrole article changes and the open list returns to ten products. Both editions retain the separate IS 10500 requirements for vending water.

## Water sampled at the vending outlet

FSSAI standard 2.10.9 identifies water offered or sold through a purification and vending system. FoSCoS places this product in category 14.1.1, under non-alcoholic beverages. Through 30 November, CTR 2.2.1(2), serial 4, gives a maximum of 10 ppm for naturally occurring saffrole in non-alcoholic beverages. This is a group-based interpretation for the dispensed water. Identify the outlet and sample in the IS 10500 section of the worksheet. For a laboratory comparison, use the same sample ID and a result on a mass basis. A value in mg/L requires a documented conversion to a mass basis. [Water Gazette, p. 4](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Gazette_Notification_Drinking_Water_05_02_2019.pdf#page=4) · [FoSCoS product list](https://fcstraining.fssai.gov.in/standard-product) · [Appendix A, category 14.1](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Appendix%20A%282%29.pdf#page=57) · [Natural toxins Gazette, p. 4](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Gazette_Notification_Crop_Contaminants_Natural_Toxic.pdf#page=4)

From 1 December 2026 the replacement 10 ppm entry requires mace or nutmeg kernel as an ingredient. Plain vending water contains neither. Its December plan therefore leaves the saffrole maximum blank. [Amendment, commencement on p. 4 and replacement on p. 5](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/272840.pdf#page=5)

## Coffee–chicory mixture

Record whether the sample is the dry roasted-ground mixture, the instant powder, the brewed drink, or an incoming coffee or chicory lot. FSSAI classifies coffee mixes with the associated beverages for food-additive purposes. The CTR beverage entry gives no basis for transferring its liquid-beverage limit to dry coffee–chicory powder. The R149 worksheet closes that option for the dry blend. Method 04A.014:2023 expressly includes ground and instant coffee–chicory mixtures for microscopic examination for adulterants. Record a qualitative finding and the sample identity. The method supplies no numerical contaminant limit. [Beverage standard, section 2.10.4](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_10_BEVERAGES_Other%20than%20Dairy%20and%20Fruits%20Vegetables%20based.pdf#page=8) · [FSSAI manual, PDF p. 37](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Manual%20of%20Methods%20of%20Analysis%20for%20Beverages%20Tea%2C%20Coffee%20and%20Chicory.pdf#page=37)

## Cocoa beans, mass, liquor, cake and powder

For whole fermented and dried beans, moisture uses a mass percentage and physical defects use a count percentage. Cocoa mass and cake have separate shell-only maxima of 1.75% and 4.5% on the alkali-free basis. The shell-and-germ maximum is 5% on fat-free dry matter for both. Acid-insoluble ash and crude fibre continue onto the second page of the table. The cocoa butter range of 47–60% applies to mass. [Bean standard, pp. 28–29](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/amendments/food-products-standards/Gazette_Notification_Standards_Fruits_FPSFA_09_08_2017.pdf#page=28) · [Mass and cake standard, pp. 13–14](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_7%20%28Sweets%20and%20Confectionary%29.pdf#page=13)

The copper row names cocoa powder and gives 70 ppm on a fat-free substance basis. Use that reference for a separately sampled, identified powder lot and record the laboratory basis. Beans, mass, liquor and pressed cake retain their own testing plans. [Metal Gazette, p. 23](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/amendments/contaminants-toxins/5f3d09f97b78aGazette_Notification_Limit_Metal_19_08_2020.pdf#page=23)
