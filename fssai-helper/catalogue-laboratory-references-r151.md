# Coffee and cocoa laboratory references

Reviewed 26 November 2026. In the product worksheet, open **Testing plan and source decisions** and select **Laboratory methods and prevention guidance**. Each entry identifies the material to sample, the purpose of the reference and its location. The same references appear in the individual and full-catalogue testing-plan downloads. Choose the coffee–chicory form in the worksheet to narrow its individual plan.

| Sample | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Coffee–chicory blend | Seven additional FSSAI methods cover moisture, total ash, acid-insoluble ash, aqueous extract, HPLC caffeine, microscopy and boiling-water solubility. The aqueous-extract entry covers ground blends. The boiling-water test covers instant blends. Record the observed material for microscopy. Use the recipe to assess the declared coffee percentage. |
| Cocoa mass, liquor or cake | Six additional FSSAI methods cover two moisture procedures, acid-insoluble ash, alkalinity of total ash, cocoa butter and crude fibre. For ash alkalinity under 2.7.8 use the moisture- and fat-free basis. The cocoa-butter composition range applies to mass or liquor. A laboratory may measure residual fat in cake for a different purpose. |
| Whole cocoa beans | Codex guidance on ochratoxin A addresses fermentation, drying and storage. Cadmium guidance begins with the growing area. Link the bean sample to its origin and record soil measurements separately. |
| Cocoa processing | Much of the ochratoxin A on whole beans can be associated with shell. Identify the sample fraction and retain the shell-removal record when interpreting results for nibs, liquor or cake. |
| Coffee before blending | The Codex coffee code addresses drying and protecting the coffee lot from rewetting. A finished coffee–chicory blend requires its own sample and applicable provision. |

The two dated testing plans include 32 analytical references, 32 laboratory-recognition entries and four Codex prevention entries. Published acceptance criteria retain their own entries. The laboratory guide has 199 entries for 26 November and 276 for 1 December. Among 533 products, nine and ten respectively still await their first product-specific domestic contaminant, toxin or residue provision.
