# Regulatory sample quantities

Regulation 2.3.1 of the Food Safety and Standards (Laboratory and Sample Analysis) Regulations, 2011 describes the approximate quantity to send to the Food Analyst or Director for official analysis. Each of the 533 worksheets and both dated testing plans now include a sampling entry. The separate table download contains all 52 statutory items. It shows both physical forms of item 46.

| Sample as supplied | Table item | Approximate quantity |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Arrowroot or sago | 16 | 250 g |
| Instant coffee–chicory mixture | 33 | 100 g |
| Roasted coffee or roasted chicory sampled separately | 32 | 500 g |
| Chocolate | 35 | 200 g |
| Sugar confectionery, chewing gum or bubble gum | 34 | 200 g |
| Natural mineral water or packaged drinking water | 47 | 4,000 mL in at least three original sealed packs |
| Canned food | 51 | Six sealed cans |

Choose the actual marketed form for products with several possible entries. The coffee worksheet uses its existing form selection. For example, the instant blend has a named quantity. A roasted coffee–chicory blend requires its own article decision. Keep sample dispatch amounts separate from the number of sample units in a microbiological plan, the amount used in an analytical method and any chemical limit.

For products without a confirmed table match, check the article and its packaging. Item 52 lists 500 g for unspecified foods. Regulation 2.3.1(4) also requires consultation with the Food Analyst about the quantity drawn. From 1 April 2027, the amended proviso directs the sampler to the applicable Food Authority guidelines. Unconfirmed products have a blank quantity in the catalogue export.
