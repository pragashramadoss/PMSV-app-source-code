# Cereal and grain microbiological testing plans

27 November 2026 · R163

The cereal export covers all 77 Chapter 2.4 catalogue entries and the fruit, vegetable and cereal flakes entry under 2.3.20. Eleven products have criteria that apply by identity. Nine need a decision on the finished form. For the remaining 58, the named form does not establish a numerical Table 9 category. Their worksheets give the product clause and the question to resolve. A scope decision does not exempt a food business from its other food safety obligations.

## Selecting a product

| Finished article or sample | What the worksheet does |
| --- | --- |
| Formulated supplements for children under 2.4.11(4) | Applies the express cross-reference to Follow Up Formula. Tables 2A and 2B, row 9, supply five process-hygiene and four food-safety criteria with their respective sampling plans. |
| Malted milk food | Shows the Chapter 2.4.11(1) criteria alongside the Table 9 criteria. Preserve the organism, portion and product variant for each entry. The combined Salmonella and Shigella requirement in the product clause and the separate Salmonella criterion in Table 9B have different test portions. |
| Malt-based foods | Shows the limits in 2.4.11(2). Malt extract has its own standard and requires separate assessment. |
| Solvent-extracted soya, groundnut, sesame, coconut and cottonseed flours | Shows each flour's three Chapter 2.4.13 microbiological requirements. The total bacterial maximum is 50,000/g, the coliform maximum is 10/g, and Salmonella must be absent in 25 g. The product clauses give no sampling-plan values for n or c. |
| Bread, instant noodles and pasta | Applies Table 9 row 3. Its safety footnote retains Salmonella and excludes Listeria monocytogenes for instant noodles and pasta. |
| Breakfast cereal, corn flakes, oat products, grain-based biscuits and wafers, papad and roasted Bengal gram flour | Requires the product identity, intended consumption and a reference to the label or specification before selecting the ready-to-eat grain category. Papad has two catalogue entries. |
| Fruit, vegetable and cereal flakes | Keeps the documented ready-to-eat grain and dehydrated fruit or vegetable categories available. The finished formulation, sampled form and applicable classification determine the choice. |
| Raw grain, milling fractions, dry ingredients and soy preparations | Records what evidence is missing for the marketed form. A raw flour used in a batter cannot inherit the finished batter's sampling plan. |

The dedicated export contains 152 criteria and scope decisions. The 31 product-standard rows for the malt products and solvent-extracted flours now appear in the microbiology section of the full testing plan. They retain their limits, units and product conditions. Individual exports retain the selected sample evidence. The full catalogue exports show public criteria and possible product forms.

## Reading Table 9

Table 9A assesses process hygiene at the end of manufacture. Table 9B addresses batch or lot acceptability at the end of manufacture and for products on the market during shelf life. Record whether a sample is a raw ingredient, ready-to-cook preparation or finished ready-to-eat food. Table 9B marks the two listed pathogen criteria as inapplicable to ready-to-cook batters and doughs. For its first row, check the specified sprouted grain, sweet corn cob or packed wet grain and direct-consumption conditions.
