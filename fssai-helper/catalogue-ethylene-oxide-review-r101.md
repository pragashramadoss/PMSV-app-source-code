# R101 · Ethylene oxide in a spice laboratory report

Reviewed on 21 November 2026. The worksheet covers the 36 marketed spice and culinary herb products already indexed in R100. All 533 products can record an actual sample of a spice ingredient. For a candy made with cinnamon, select the cinnamon ingredient only when the laboratory tested that ingredient. Results for the candy as a composite require a separate article and method review.

## Reading the Indian sources

At its 43rd meeting, the Food Authority approved item 3.3 on ethylene oxide (EO) in spices. The recorded discussion addresses both the use of EO in treating spices and traces in the spice itself. The decision supplies no numerical acceptance limit. A report below a laboratory threshold leaves any smaller amount unresolved. Treatment records still matter. The helper keeps culinary-herb classification open where the wording needs interpretation. [Food Authority minutes, 2 February 2024, item 3.3, PDF p. 8](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/43rd%20FA%20Minutes.pdf#page=8)

FSSAI approved method FSSAI.OM.EO.001.2024 for free EO and 2-chloroethanol (2-CE) in several food matrices including spices and herbs. The 16 October 2024 order gives the implementation date. The method gives a factor of 0.55 to express native 2-CE as EO by mass. A 2-CE finding calls for an investigation of its origin. A combined result alone does not identify which component was detected. [Approval and matrix scope, PDF pp. 1–2](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Method%20for%20ETO%20and%202-CE.pdf#page=2) · [Calculation, annex p. 7, PDF p. 8](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Method%20for%20ETO%20and%202-CE.pdf#page=8)

For exports, Spices Board Circular 21/2024–25 issued revised guidance. Record the country and the current destination provision for the exact article. The assessment retains the destination reference. The circular's examples do not provide an Indian domestic threshold. [Spices Board circular and revised guidance, PDF pp. 1–3](https://www.indianspices.com/sites/default/files/EtO%20SoP%20v.pdf#page=2)

## Interpreting a report

In **Product finder**, open the **Product worksheet**, then **Pesticide residues** and the spice review. Choose the sampled article and select **Ethylene oxide and 2-chloroethanol**. Record the lot, intended market, treatment evidence and laboratory report. The lab may give two individual results or a combined total as EO.

| Example report | Analytical result in the helper |
| --- | --- |
| Free EO 0.010 mg/kg and native 2-CE 0.040 mg/kg | EO sum = 0.032 mg/kg. |
| Free EO below 10 µg/kg and native 2-CE 0.040 mg/kg | 0.022 ≤ EO sum < 0.032 mg/kg. The worksheet preserves whether the lab reported non-detection, a detected trace or an unspecified less-than qualifier. |
| Free EO 0.010 mg/kg and the 2-CE contribution already expressed as 0.022 mg/kg of EO | EO sum = 0.032 mg/kg. The conversion factor has already been applied by the lab. |
| Only a combined total of 0.032 mg/kg as EO | The lab-reported total is retained. The component contributions remain unresolved. |
| Free EO reported, 2-CE unreported | The free EO finding remains visible. A complete sum requires the missing result. |

Bounds assume non-negative concentrations. They describe the reported analytical information. They make no allowance for measurement uncertainty and do not establish a release decision. A “detected below LOQ” result retains its detection flag even when a numerical bound is shown. Enter the laboratory's actual threshold in the reported unit. A bare zero or an ND with no threshold requires clarification.

**Save EO assessment to the panel** retains one finding per article, lot, report and intended market. Separate reports and destinations retain separate lines. The product brief, the results CSV and the full 533-product register carry the source and available evidence. The 120-column register appends six fields to R100 and contains no sample results when first downloaded.
