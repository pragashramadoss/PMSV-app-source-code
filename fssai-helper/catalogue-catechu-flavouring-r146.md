# Edible catechu supplied as a natural flavouring

Reviewed 26 November 2026. The catalogue now has 522 products with at least one partial or conditional finished-product contaminant, toxin or residue source. Eleven products still await their first such source.

The Indian standard for edible catechu, FPSFA 2.11.2, identifies a dried aqueous extract of *Acacia catechu* heartwood. Regulation 3.3.1(1) covers plant-derived flavour preparations obtained by physical processes and supplied for their taste or odour. The product name alone does not establish the technological function or permission for its intended use. Match the supplier specification, process, intended Indian food application and sampled lot. The US FDA inventory independently identifies a flavouring use for black catechu extract. Indian requirements govern this assessment. | Metal | Published maximum | When the worksheet applies it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead | 10 mg/kg on dry matter | A separately sampled edible extract is documented as a natural flavouring. The laboratory confirms the lot and dry-matter basis. |
| Copper | 30 mg/kg for the applicable solid | The Gazette groups “flavourings/pectin liquid” in one entry. Record the basis for including the supplied solid under that wording and confirm the as-supplied mass basis. Without that evidence the comparison stays open. |

The copper entry gives no dry-matter qualifier. An ordinary edible ingredient has a separate assessment. Applying a residual maximum requires a record identifying the lot and ruling out named articles. A finished pan masala, blended flavour, industrial cutch or different plant material falls outside these two catechu checks. The scheduled 1 December 2026 amendment leaves both cited flavouring entries intact.
