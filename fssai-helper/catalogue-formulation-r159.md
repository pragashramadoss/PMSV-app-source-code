# Product-specific formulation rules

27 November 2026 · R159

The formulation lookup now follows the reference in the product standard for eight catalogue entries. It is available in the product worksheet, individual and catalogue testing plans, and the **Export product formulation rules** CSV. The CSV distinguishes published additive use levels from contaminant limits. Sampling quantities and the domestic contaminant-source register remain separate.

| Product | Provision | Formulation check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk protein concentrate | 2.1.21(3) | Use milk powder additive provisions. The previous lookup followed the condensed-milk category. |
| Whey protein concentrate | 2.1.22(3) | Use the whey powder provisions. For example, the 100 mg/kg benzoyl peroxide entry carries the infant-food exclusion in Note 147. |
| Cow or buffalo colostrum | 2.1.23(3)(a) | Liquid colostrum permits no food additives. |
| Colostrum powder (same catalogue entry) | 2.1.23(3)(b) | Confirm a stabilizing, emulsifying or antioxidant function and a milk powder provision. Check individual and group conditions. |
| Dairy permeate powders | 2.1.24(3) | Verify the specific additive permission. The standard addresses pH-adjusting processing aids separately under GMP. |
| Royal jelly | 2.8.3(3)(a) | Both types must be free of additives. The type concerns bee feed. |
| Coconut milk powder | 2.3.63(1), (3) | Added colour and flavour are prohibited. Maltodextrin and sodium caseinate are optional ingredients. |
| Cocoa mass, liquor or cake | 2.7.8(c) | Added colouring, flavour and fat other than cocoa butter are prohibited. The existing cocoa additive worksheet gives substance-specific conditions. |
| Dry coffee–chicory mixtures | 2.10.4 | The standard specifies the coffee and chicory ingredients. Check ground, instant and decaffeinated forms against their respective descriptions. |

## BHA and BHT in powder

In **Food additives and processing aids**, identify the sample and report. For colostrum, select **Colostrum Powder** under Product variant. The milk powder table lists BHA at 100 mg/kg and BHT at 200 mg/kg, both on a fat basis. Record whether the lab reports mg/kg fat or mg/kg whole powder. A whole-powder result requires the fat percentage from the same sample. For example, 20 mg/kg BHA in powder with 20% fat gives 100 mg/kg fat. Comparison uses the unrounded result.

Note 196 covers BHA, BHT and propyl gallate used singly or in combination. The calculator handles the single-antioxidant case after the reviewer confirms the formula. Save the worksheet to retain the referenced result. The product export includes it. The bulk export contains general rules and source notes.
