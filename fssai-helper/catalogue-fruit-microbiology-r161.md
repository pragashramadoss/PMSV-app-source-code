# Fruit and vegetable microbiological testing plans

27 November 2026 · R161

Twelve product worksheets now link the finished-food standard to the process and sample form in FSSAI Appendix B, Tables 4A and 4B. The catalogue export lists the available decisions. On an individual worksheet, record the process specification or batch reference to generate the plan for that product.

| Product standard | Decision for the sample |
| --- | --- |
| Fruit juices, 2.3.6 | Distinguish minimally processed packaged juice, pasteurized juice, other thermal processing and retort processing. The pasteurized option retains Listeria by default. |
| Vegetable juices, 2.3.7 | Use the documented process. The pasteurized route retains Listeria across the pH range. |
| Tomato juice, 2.3.8 | Confirm the juice standard and heat process. An acidic tomato juice retains Listeria on the pasteurized route. |
| Carbonated fruit beverages, 2.3.30 | Confirm the beverage identity and choose whether to assess the optional low-pH exclusion. |
| Mango pulp or purée, 2.3.11, and other fruit pulp or purée, 2.3.12 | Identify the thermal or retort process and the liquid or non-liquid sample form. |
| Concentrated fruit pulp or purée and concentrated vegetable pulp or purée, 2.3.13 | Check both the preservation process and the finished concentrate's form. |
| Tamarind pulp, purée and concentrate, 2.3.18 | Document the preservation process and sampled form. Check the fermentation footnote when relevant. |
| Canned tomatoes, 2.3.3A | Confirm the thermal process from the process record. Select retort only when that record establishes retorting. |
| Thermally processed tomato purée and paste, 2.3.14 | Match the process and distinguish liquid from non-liquid presentations. |
| Thermally processed vegetable soups, 2.3.5 | Confirm that the sampled soup matches the liquid thermal or retort category. |

**Listeria and pH.** Footnote 4 permits an optional exclusion for pasteurized fruit juices and carbonated fruit beverages when pH is below 4.4. The worksheet applies it only when the user chooses that option and enters the measured pH and a supporting reference. An eligible sample at pH 4.39 can qualify. A sample at pH 4.40 retains the test. Changing the result clears its supporting reference. Changing the process clears the earlier process and pH evidence. Individual exports record the decision, while catalogue exports list the public criteria.

**Test portions.** Appendix B uses millilitres in place of grams for liquid products. The selected route preserves the table's sampling values and test portions. A worksheet whose process or sampled form falls outside the listed choices records the outstanding assessment.

Both dated exports cover all 533 products. These twelve worksheets address microbiological criteria. The separate chemical source register still flags products awaiting their first exact source.
