### Provisional official category lookup for nine Ayurveda recipes

All nine R29 Ayurveda Category A source records have blank FCS. The official **1 September 2025 FSSAI FoSCoS Order** explicitly lists **102.1 – Ayurveda Aahara Category A** under manufacturer KoB Ayurveda Aahara. The R51 audit now records **102.1 as an official source anchor for all nine**; it does NOT change the original R29 field, master product categorization or claim a specific manufacturer holds the appropriate Central Licence. The **two recipe source conflicts 14.6/14.7 remain unresolved**.

**Specific R29 mapping problem flagged: recipe 14.5 Dugdha Kupika** has *FSSR 2.1.17* in its historical field. The official **2017 dairy-products Gazette** specifically names **2.1.17 Standard for Cheese and Cheese Products**. The recipe name refers to a stuffed fried cottage-cheese dumpling. Thus 2.1.17 is potentially a **cheese component standard**, but is **not automatically a finished-product identity standard for the cooked Ayurveda recipe**. Hold the whole-product FSSR/CTR decision pending actual formulation, lawful recipe/class and legal review. This is not a claim that a component-standard obligation disappears.

### Why 12 blank FSSR records under FPC 18 must not be force-filled

The **20 July 2021 Indian Sweets/Snacks Direction** defines 18.1/18.2 categories for licensing and restricts FPC 18 applicability for already standardized products in mapped FPC 01–14. Paragraph 4(b) and **Annexure II Tables 1/2** map **additive provisions** to particular food categories; they do **not** turn those mapped numbers into a single blanket standardized finished-product FSSR clause. Clauses 4(f)–4(i) also require actual category and base-food contaminant, process microbiology, aflatoxin/DON and pesticide checks. R34's category-specific mapping is linked for each of the 12 cases.

### The other 13 blank FSSR records

These are **two interim dried seed routes**, **three mouth-freshener classes**, **seven category-99 ingredient/additive/mixture routes**, and **one formulated children's supplement**. They need actual ingredient identity, source/grade, intended use, legally operative classification and specific regulatory provisions; no invented generic FSSR clause was inserted.
