# Microbiological testing plans for preserved fruit and vegetable products

27 November 2026 · R162

The fruit and vegetable export now covers 55 catalogue products. This release adds 43 process-selection worksheets. For 20 of these products, the previous plan only asked the user to establish an Appendix B category. The 27 November and 1 December editions each list 1,262 fruit and vegetable criteria and decisions. A single batch normally uses one documented route.

## Selecting a plan

| Products | Evidence for the worksheet |
| --- | --- |
| Squash, crush, cordial, fruit syrup or sharbat, barley water | Confirm the fruit-based identity and whether the undiluted finished sample is liquid. The liquid preserved route uses CFU/mL and a 25 mL qualitative portion where those units apply. |
| Fruit nectar, ready-to-serve fruit drink, ginger cocktail | Record the product base and final process. For ginger cocktail, a juice base and an essence or oleoresin base need separate consideration. |
| Preserved industrial fruit and vegetable pulps, purées and concentrates | Use the form of the sampled product before dilution. Select grams for a non-liquid sample or millilitres for a liquid sample. |
| Canned or thermally processed fruit, fruit cocktail, vegetables, vegetable meals and chestnuts | Record the thermal treatment. Confirm retorting from the process record before choosing the retort row. |
| Jam, jelly, marmalade, fruit cheese, chutneys, murabba and candied products | Identify the actual preserving step. A sugar level, coating or cooking step alone does not establish an Appendix B category. |
| Fruit and vegetable sauces, tomato ketchup, chilli sauce, ginger paste and culinary pastes | Establish the finished food's base, process and sampled form. Harissa under 2.3.58 is preserved by thermal treatment only. |
| Fruit bars or fruit toffee, soup powders, raisins and ground dried fungi | Establish the dried fruit or vegetable identity. Select the dried product as supplied. For fruit toffee, confirm the fruit-pulp sheet described in 2.3.19. |
| Fruit, vegetable and mixed pickles, olives and processed fungi | Record the preservation method. The individual worksheet distinguishes a documented yeast or mould fermentation from acidification, brining and heat preservation. The fermented fungi standard specifically describes lactic acid fermentation. |

## Process hygiene and lot acceptability

Table 4A assesses process hygiene at the end of manufacture. An unsatisfactory result calls for investigation and corrective action. Table 4B sets food safety criteria for the lot at the end of manufacture and in the market throughout shelf life. The worksheet identifies the table for each criterion and the CSV explains when it applies. [Appendix B, pages 35–36](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/compendium/food-products-standards/Appendix%20B%281%29.pdf#page=35)

For fruit, vegetable and mixed pickles and for table olives, the routine preserved-product plan retains the yeast and mould count. Where the fermentation actually involves yeast or mould, select the corresponding option and enter the batch or process record. The individual plan then omits only that count under footnote 3. The remaining criteria and sampling values still apply. An unknown starter, an acid addition or a lactic fermentation alone does not establish the exception. [Table 4A](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/compendium/food-products-standards/Appendix%20B%281%29.pdf#page=30) · [Gazetted amendment](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Gazette_Notification_Fruits_Vegetables_04_04_2018.pdf)

The two earlier low-pH Listeria decisions remain restricted to the qualifying pasteurized fruit juice and carbonated fruit beverage routes. A batch record and pH evidence stay with the individual worksheet. The full catalogue exports contain public criteria and possible routes. [Appendix B, footnotes 3 and 4](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/compendium/food-products-standards/Appendix%20B%281%29.pdf#page=35)
