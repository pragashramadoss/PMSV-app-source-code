# Microbiological testing for spices, herbs and seasonings

Reviewed 27 November 2026. The two dated testing plans include 43 Chapter 2.9 spice, herb and seasoning entries. For 14, the named product identifies a numerical Table 3 category. For 28, the reviewer selects a documented marketed form. One product, asafoetida, requires an individual classification before applying numerical criteria. Common salt, double-fortified salt and salt substitutes are outside this export.

## Which food was sampled?

| Named product or sample | Application in the Helper |
| --- | --- |
| Fenugreek seed or saffron | Choose the dried whole material or the powder made from it. The fenugreek seed standard does not cover fresh fenugreek leaves. |
| Black, white and green peppers under 2.9.15 | Confirm the dry whole, cracked, crushed or ground style. The standard describes green pepper produced by removing moisture from green berries. |
| Poppy seed, aniseed, ajowan, celery seed, tejpat and star anise | Apply the dried category to the article specified in the product standard. Confirm the identity separately for a paste, fresh herb or another preparation. |
| Dry and paste seasonings | Confirm the spice or herb ingredients and the actual form. For a semi-solid paste, record the applicable preservation process before selecting the wet or preserved category. |
| Natural Hing or Hingra and compounded asafoetida | Record the resin or compound, formula and process. Chapter 2.9 requires Table 3 review, but the name alone cannot establish a particular processing category. |
| Spice oleoresins | Use the extracted category. Its limits and the permitted number of units between m and M differ from those for a ground spice. |

## How to check a report

Select the food form when required, enter its specification or batch reference, then choose the organism. Enter the lot, the laboratory report identifier and all five individual results in the stated unit. For Enterobacteriaceae in spice oleoresin, m is 10 cfu/g, M is 100 cfu/g and c is 1. One result above 10 and at or below 100 is acceptable if all other units are at or below 10. Two results above 10, or any result above 100, are unsatisfactory. The worksheet compares exact counts and sends qualified results such as “<10” for individual review.

Table 3A assesses process hygiene at the end of manufacture. Table 3B assesses food safety at the end of manufacture and during market shelf life. The result shown is for one laboratory set and one organism. Regulatory sampling uses three laboratory sets. The public catalogue describes the possible forms. Individual exports and briefs retain the selected form and report.

## Correction in R166

Table 3B lists Salmonella, sulphite-reducing clostridia and Bacillus cereus for wet-ground, preserved or pickled spices. R165 incorrectly attributed a Listeria entry to the compendium. The corrected worksheet includes all four Table 3A process-hygiene criteria and the three Table 3B food-safety criteria for this form.
