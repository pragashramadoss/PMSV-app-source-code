# Thermally processed fruit and vegetable packs

Reviewed 20 November 2026. This review adds usable pack measurements to the R66 source records for catalogue entries 146 and 185. The entries cover fruit under FSSAI 2.3.1 and vegetables under 2.3.3. The original 2011 Gazette, the relevant fruit and vegetable amendments and the consolidated chapter were checked together.

## Drained weight

Record the drained solids and actual net contents in grams from the same container. The percentage is 100 times drained solids divided by net contents. Net contents exclude container tare. A declared quantity on the label cannot establish the measured net weight.

| Food and packing style | FSSAI provision | Minimum drained weight, % of actual net contents | Minimum for 400 g net |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fruit, liquid pack | 2.3.1(2)(i) | 50% | 200 g |
| Fruit, solid pack | 2.3.1(2)(ii) | 70% | 280 g |
| Mushrooms in a liquid other than sauce | 2.3.3(1)(i)(a) | 50% | 200 g |
| Green beans, carrots, peas, sweet corn or baby corn in a liquid other than sauce | 2.3.3(1)(i)(b) | 50% | 200 g |
| Edible fungi or vegetables, including baked beans, packed in sauce | 2.3.3(1)(i)(c) | 25% | 100 g |
| Other vegetables in a liquid other than sauce | 2.3.3(1)(i)(d) | 50% | 200 g |
| Vegetables, solid pack | 2.3.3(1)(ii) | 70% | 280 g |

The Tenth Amendment, notified 29 December 2020, permits cherries with stems and requires corresponding labelling. For those cherries, remove the stems before determining drained weight. For example, a liquid pack with 400 g actual net contents and 195 g drained, de-stemmed cherries yields 48.75%, below the 50% minimum. The Helper requests a new weight when the preparation changes. [Fruit amendment, PDF p. 29](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/amendments/food-products-standards/5fedb66ac1b15Gazette_Notification_FPS_Tenth_amendment_31_12_2020.pdf#page=29)

The Fifth Amendment, notified 15 November 2021, replaced the former mushroom-in-sauce row with the broader category of edible fungi or vegetables, including baked beans, packed in sauce. For the same 400 g net contents, 100 g drained meets this row. The pack description determines which row applies. [Vegetable amendment, PDF p. 19](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/amendments/food-products-standards/619b553646e10Gazette_Notification_Oil_Flour_22_11_2021.pdf#page=19)

FSSAI’s 2016 fruit and vegetable methods manual describes draining ordinary canned fruits and vegetables on a sieve with 2.5 mm square openings for two minutes. The procedure for products in sauce calls for 0.3 mm square openings, washing off adhering material and a five-minute drain. The Helper uses finished measurements from the applicable procedure. [Drained-weight method, PDF pp. 5–6](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Manual_Fruits_Veg_25_05_2016.pdf#page=5)

## Container fill and label review

Under 2.3.1(3) and 2.3.3(2), a rigid pack must be at least 90% full by volume. Use the full capacity measured with distilled water at 20 °C. A 300 mL rigid container, including glass, therefore needs at least 270 mL of product. Flexible packs still have a drained-weight check. The separate standards for fruit salad or cocktail and for canned tomatoes have their own calculations. [Fruit article, PDF p. 1](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_3_Fruit_Vegetable_products.pdf#page=1) · [Vegetable article, PDF p. 6](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Chapter%202_3_Fruit_Vegetable_products.pdf#page=6)

Both articles call for the packing medium and its strength on the label. The vegetable article also specifies the names of the vegetables and the percentage range of each. The report records label observations separately from measured weight and fill. The two catalogue records retain their R66 history and open contaminant decisions.
